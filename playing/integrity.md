# Integrity in the pocket

You, player, are going to spend a lot of your time inside psychic pockets throughout this game. But what does it mean to _be_ inside a pocket? What does it mean to play at being inside a pocket?

## What is a psychic pocket?

You may have already gleaned some obvious tenants from reading elsewhere: pockets are mental spaces, liminal, unreal, and subject to the whims and powers of anyone who enters them (which, let's be level, is no one outside you and your cohorts).

Are they dreams? No, though their spaces sometimes fold together with the non-euclidean joinery that one finds in a dream.

So they must be real? Well, not that either. They are part of this world, but also outside of it. Little baubles of reality unto themselves.

Could I tell you exactly what a pocket is? I certainly could. But I won't. That thieves any opportunity for you to discover it on your own. Ultimately it doesn't matter if it's a tapestry of gossamer dreamings or a dimensional spark of new and electric existence. It's not the makings of a pocket that truly matters. What well and truly matters is whether you and the other players play that space with integrity.

## What happens to you inside a pocket?

To know how to play a pocket we must start by asking two questions: what's really happening to our characters while they're inside, and why does any of what happens in there even matter to them to begin with?

That second question might sound silly- you're playing this game to find out what happens, aren't you? It must matter to you! But put yourself, for a moment, in the space of a traveler to imaginary places. It shouldn't be difficult, you're a traveler of the unreal already. How often in your real life do you worry that a character in a video game might not love you back? Reading novels, does the inability to reach through the pages and serve food to a starving mother and children make your nerves ache?  Have you ever feared that to die in your dreams would kill you in real life?

A persons' natural instinct is to insulate themselves from unreality. You will not die in your dream. A book cannot be served food. The video game, axiomatic to its code, cannot reciprocate your love. You are safe. Safe from harm, and from guilt, and sadness.

I can give you a clear assurance: pockets are not physical spaces, and you are safe. Safe from the possibility of death and injury at least. Your journey here, by principle and covenant, is to find out how your time in these pockets changes you; not to find out what untimely demise claims you at the end.

Another assurance: that promise of safety isn't there for your protection. It's a mere ordinance. You are safe from harm. The pocket can still harm you.

Why the pedantry? Because of that simple question: why does any of what happens in a pocket even matter ? Pockets _are_ non-real. You could permit the harm and guilt and fear to pass over and through you. Though less likely, you could do the same with awe and wonder and epiphany. What would be left to you at the end save the statement of the journey alone: you visited psychic unreality- the universe of a person's life; the world at their innermost center- and then you returned.

## Playing with integrity

Make your characters treat each pocket as if it were real. Not just real. Indistinguishable from reality. Indistinguishable from the experience of living and breathing and feeling and spasms of rapture and thralls of suffering and cradling embrace of wonder. That's all I'm asking of you.

I am asking you to tell me as you fall asleep, "I go now to dream, where nothing is real". And then, once dreaming, to tell me, "I know that I am dreaming, and all of this is real, and all of it matters."

You might be tempted, in the pocket, as you watch the impossible unfold into the world, as you observe mass and shape and absence intercourse, to turn to the person next to you and whisper, "I get what's happening here, this is one of them mindfucks. Well I've a mind like a steel trap."  You might be tempted to tell the ghost of your late daughter that they not here, that they're dead, in a box beneath the cold earth, and you aren't falling for it. You might be tempted to throw a punch at the brother of the deceased- not because they're an ornery cuss and they deserve it (which they certainly do)- but because you fear no recourse from a figment.

Fight these temptations. They arise from the part of you that hopes to barricade itself from uncertainty, for your character's safety, for your own safety.  The excuse is right there, it's always right there: "the pocket isn't real, this doesn't matter". There is no integrity in the excuse.

As the author, I could coerce the concern through mechanics: for harm, for the terror of a short drop and a sudden stop.  But that's not integrity either, not the game I want to make, nor the game I want you to play. Besides, I trust you, and that you're here for this experience as much as I am. It's all a matter of seeing to it that we understand each other about what that experience means. And then sticking to it. That's what integrity means.
