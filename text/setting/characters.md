# Characters

We call all the actors in play "characters". What's an actor? Anyone who takes the stage and participates in the scene. The players' characters, the MC's characters, the anthropomorphization and innate characteristics of the pocket, and the terrible afflictions that seek to harm all of them.

These following terms will help us describe two boundaries: who is in control, and what are they in control of?

Most often you'll see the text use generic terms like "someone", "anyone", "person", and so on. These are all interchangeable with "a character"; not only for people, but afflictions, monsters, horrors, and so on. If you see a specific term used (ex: "affliction"), it was invoked intentionally and the rule only apply to that kind of character.

## Players

The people at your table divvy up into two groups: [players](/text/playing/the_players.md), and the [Mistress of Chimeras](/text/playing/the_players.md). The MC isn't a player, technically, but this text doesn't put up much effort to examine that at every mention. Many times it's easier to refer to "a player", even if that might end up pointing to the MC.

For example, you'll see plays hand decision making over to the target of the move by calling out "their player". _"Or a miss, their player will tell you what happens next"_, eg. If a player made that play against one of the MC's NPCs, then the MC is "their player".

## Player Characters (PCs)

Every player is in control of one, and only one, PC. The MC is the only person at the table who doesn't control a PC. They have enough bookkeeping to do already.

Controlling the character means you're in charge of everything they say, do, think and believe, and all the ways we see them act those things out. You are that character's author and get the final say on anything they do or don't do. The rules (and, by extension, the MC) will set you up with constraints, relationships, and emotions to explore. _How_ you engage with all of that is up to you.

## Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

Any character who isn't a PC must be an NPC. These characters are all ostensibly under control of the MC; after all, the MC does generally control the world outside the PCs.

However, "Non-player" doesn't mean the players will never control an NPC. Some phases of play (like [downtime](/text/downtime/downtime.md)) are great opportunities for players to pick up an NPC for a spell. Whomever steps into the role gets to take full control of that character, and counts as "their player", until they step out of the role again.

## Pocket Denizens

### Visitors

The people who enter a pocket from the real world are called Visitors. That's you. And, to be quite frank, only you. The transgression of entering into someone else's pocket is hardly common. Only a handful of people in the world even know the possibility and of those a scant few ever undertake the journey.

Whenever ever you see the term `Visitor`, the text is almost certainly talking about only two potential characters: the PCs themselves, or any NPC who was brought along into the pocket at their side.

### Figments

All the people living in the pocket are Figments. Are they real people? Perhaps not to visitors like yourself; certainly not when peeking in from the outside. But then, all the pocket seems imaginary to someone on the outside.

Everything on the inside is as real as can be. These figments have flesh and blood in as much measure as you. They were born and raised in this place. They cultivated the land, built their houses on it, and have fought and loved and worshipped as much as any real person. Truly, you, visitor from the beyond, are more supernatural than they are.

### Afflictions

[Afflictions](/text/setting/afflictions.md) are the manifestation of some trouble or evil which has beset the pocket or its person. They aren't figments because they aren't borne from the pocket. But they aren't simply visiting either. Think of them more like a curse or a haunting or an unforgivable grievance. Anything which can leave a wound or a scar on a person's psyche, including any malevolent force that wishes to manipulate or assault, has the potential to afflict a pocket.
