# Characters

Everyone in the pocket is a character. The rest of the terms expose two aspects: who authors control of that character, and how the character should get presented by look, location, context, and etc.

More often you'll see generic terms like "someone", "anyone", "person", and so on. These are all interchangeable with "character" and should be applied universally; not only to people, but to afflictions, monsters, and horrors. If you see a specific term used (ex: "affliction"), it was invoked intentionally and the effects only apply to that kind of character.

## Players

The people at your table divvy into two groups: [players](/text/playing/the_players.md), and the [Mistress of Chimeras](/text/playing/the_players.md). The MC isn't a player, technically, but this text doesn't put up much effort to be exacting at every turn. Many times it's easier to refer to "a player", even if that might end up pointing to the MC.

For example, you may see a play call out "their player". "Their player will tell you what happens next", eg. If a PC made the play against an NPC, then the MC is "their player". The NPC's player, that is. They're in control right now, so they make the decision.

## Player Characters (PCs)

Every player controls one PC, generated by following the [character creation](/text/chargen/basics.md) guide. The MC is the only person at the table who doesn't create and take control of a PC. They have enough on their plate already.

Controlling the character means you're in charge of everything they say and do and all the ways we see them onscreen. You are that character's author and get the final say on anything they do or don't do. There are some small exceptions: the MC and rules may ask you to change your seeming (how you look and present yourself), and some plays will give you a feeling or activity to explore. But that's all. The rest is up to you.

## Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

Any character who isn't a PC must be an NPC.  Most of the time the MC controls all of these characters. However, "non-player" doesn't mean the players never control one. Certain parts of the game (like [downtime](/text/playing/downtime.md)) are great opportunities for players to pick up an NPC for a spell. Whomever steps in takes full control of that character until they put them down again.
