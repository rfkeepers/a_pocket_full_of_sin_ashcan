# America, 1900

## Modern Sophistications

Immigration peaks inside urban and coastal locales where ethnic traditions mingle and thrive. Feminism swells as women look to political legitimacy through the suffrage playment. Farmers abandon heritage and rural living both, turning instead to cities for work. The mixing pots of the new era churn within cities while rural life reduces: dark, distant and fallow.

City streets scintillate with electric lamps, though fewer than three percent of houses are wired for electric power. In eight short years the Model-T will overtake transportation, but we're not there yet; the first car purchase was a mere two years prior. Typewriters evolve to their modern shape and women take the field as the new stenographer. Horses still power farmstead, but now in conjunction with mechanical reapers and threshers while the tractor trundles slowly into frame.

Invention and technology arouse imagination.  Narratives dance around new devices. Physicists continue to unveil ray and radiation, proof of a universe full of invisible energies not yet captured. Medicine tampers with substances: radium for a healthy glow, opium for coughs, morphine to sleep through the night, arsenic to fight the infection.

Entertainment is alive and human. Circuses travel town to town. The wealthy elite enjoy opera and symphony in theaters while the poor frequent dance halls and bars. Journalism and sensationalism battle for control of the presses while readers gorge on wild pulp fantasies. Film ensorcels the public. Kinetoscope parlors and nickelodeons introduce radical new ideas of reality, blurring the lines between live experience and imaginary figments.

## Seething Corruption

Nativism rears up in retaliation against immigration. Americans eye foreigners in the streets like wolves, fangs out and growling. Antisemitism grows through yellow journalism and jews are accused of seeking to undermine "the working man".

Segregation entrenches itself along streets and corridors. Anti-black sentiment thrives among white citizens. Race riots erupt in New Orleans and New York; Atlanta, Springfield, and Slocum will follow soon. Communities come together to perpetrate acts of lynching and torture. Southern trees bear a strange fruit, blood on the leaves and blood at the root, bodies hung near train lines so that passengers may bear witness to the testaments of racism.

Crime overtakes where feckless protectors and judiciaries are unable or unwilling to keep pace with the violence. Homicide rates double or triple in the largest cities. The severity of punishment unevenly favors white nationals, if punishment is metered out at all. Only about 20 percent of criminals see prosecution. The temperance playment continues to gain favor and when it does, organized crime is set to take the advantage.

## Quarrels over the Mind

Little is truly known about neurosis or mental illness. Spirituality and superstition thrive among the masses.

Asylums overflow with patients. Women in particular, admitted by husbands and kin for failing to live within socially expected confines. Patients are regularly sterilized to prevent further spread of "degeneracy". The public pays to observe the patients.

Psychology is a nascent science, scrutinized and chastened by the institutions of its elders. Widespread curiosity and private donations press researchers for proof of the paranormal: telepathy, communication with the dead, hypnosis. Some psychologists hustle to prove the mind, others to prove the supernatural. Both walk a sharp line between professional legitimacy and public interest.

Mary Baker Eddy’s church of Christian Science, a fomenting religious playment of mind-cures, professes the mind as reality and the mortal body a mere distorted projection. Healers of the church eschew conventional medicine to focus on metaphysical correction of the spirit. Horatio Dresser's playment of New Thought declares the internal human spirit divine and portrays bodily sickness as a corruption in the mind.

> The trouble is in the mind, for the body is only the house for the mind to dwell in [...] Therefore, if your mind had been deceived by some invisible enemy into a belief, you have put it into the form of a disease, with or without your knowledge. By my theory or truth, I come in contact with your enemy, and restore you to health and happiness. This I do partly mentally, and partly by talking till I correct the wrong impression and establish the Truth, and the Truth is the cure.
>
> Phineas Quimby

## In The Game

The game exists in America at the turn of the century. America at a new era, at the crux of superstition and science. A country with its fill of problems and solutions alike. Where paranormal belief is healthy and thriving, invigorated, not suppressed, by the modern era. Where the cities alight bright and electric next to moonlit, organic countrysides.

What has been described above is not what the game is _about_. This is not a game for historical accuracy and you are not tasked to replicate any of it. Certainly not the disempowerment of women and minorities, the xenophobia and racism, nor the mistreatment and misunderstanding of neurodivergence. That scenery only decorates the stage, and like any set of props can be rearranged, repainted, or replayd without changing the fundamental setting: a time and place where you sin eaters might affect people in a most supernatural fashion.
