# About the meal

Every arc begins with the Seven Course Meal. Here the players get introduced to their employer, take on the contract, investigate clues, uncover the first secrets, prepare for- and even journey into- the psychic pocket.

Your employers all invariably share a couple things in common: they want the best for their loved ones; they think of you as a tool and a means to an end- not as a friend or benefactor; they know there are skeletons in the closet, they want those secrets to stay hidden, and the chance that you might reveal any of their sordid history makes you as dangerous as you are necessary.

## The ritual

In time long passed the meal was simple: a bite of bread and dram of ale delivered over a body, a quick prayer and a coin for the trouble. Where that turned to rigid etiquette is anyone's guess. There's much to the contemporary process, but three constants remain intact through the passage of time.

First, it is always a meal. All come together in communion for the act, and stay present until the deed is done. Food settles the stomach and calms the nerves. An essential preparation for the drink.

Second, the loved one, the sinner, lies in the center, and every plate first passes over their body. Thus they do not consume, but they do partake of the meal. As present and essential as the food itself.

Third, there is always a drink. The alcohol matters but little. It is the dose of psychotropic potion secreted into the each libation that separates body from psyche and transports our guests into the pocket.

## How to serve

The meal is a structured phase separated into seven parts, each represented by one plate of the meal. The first five plates give the players a specific set of questions to ask and bonuses to choose. The main course sends players into the psychic pocket- taking players out of the meal and into the next phase of play. Finally, the party returns for a brief dessert and to end the contract.

Think of this phase like a cinematic cross-cut. We begin with the characters sitting at the dinner table, some to partake in a ritual that transports them into the pocket, others attend out of propriety, concern, or pitiable malice. With each new plate the scene leaps to the recent past: uneasy handshakes, uncertain glances, a peek through the keyhole of a locked door, a shakeup in an alley, all to wheedle clues out of the employers and their suspicious associates.

This is not a time for making moves or checking success. Paint these moments however you want. Step into character and have a scene or talk about it as playwrights plotting a rehearsal. The fundamentals are concrete: the players ask questions, learn answers, swallow their mouthful and await the next plate.
