# About Moves

## Powered By The Apocalypse

This game is a [PBtA](http://apocalypse-world.com/pbta/games/find) gig, and further attributes much of its legacy from [Under Hollow Hills](https://lumpley.games/under-hollow-hills-about-the-game/). Most details from that framework hold true even here: the conversation, the moves (or plays, if you're coming from UHH), and all the intertwining of the two.

Any differences you find throughout this game are often explained on the page, or alongside the mechanics or space, where they differ. This section below constitutes a brief for the general concepts and broad strokes that are peculiar to this game and its intended experience.

## Making moves

Moves are the means by which players push and pull at the fiction, giving characters capacity to learn, communicate, and struggle. Each one is a self-contained little mechanic, specific to its own purpose, with a tidy marker for when they get used and what happens as a result.

Players are encouraged to call out their moves by name and intent. There's no necessary order between fiction and call; you can describe what you're doing and then call out its move, or the reverse, or even say just one then ask for help from the other. Other players may call out your move for you, if they think you missed it. Most often the MC will do that to help you out.

To use a move the character has to do what it says. You can't solely speak its name then skip to the consequences. Moreover, if a character does what the move says then they're doing the move.

## Handling gaps

Every move serves to elicit a particular mood or motive. The list is finite, and it isn't difficult for a character to take an action where no move fits. These gaps are intentional. If you land in one, first see if any other move fits your intent (eg: you're not _throwing a punch_- that's not a move here- but maybe you're _Barging In_, or maybe you're getting into a _Fracas_).

When all else fails the table should fall back to the conversation. Talk about what's a stake in this moment: what you want, and where the other players are invested. No rule states that the conversation cannot be enough. Moves only help guide the direction.
