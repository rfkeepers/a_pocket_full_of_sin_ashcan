# Anchors & Bindings

Anchors are the ways in which a person holds fast to their beliefs, ideas and individuality. Even someone who hasn't pondered much will secure themselves to some tenant or another. Even should that berthing be all the worse for their life. At least they won't be caught adrift. It's those buoyant few without mooring who get swept out by a current or dashed upon the indecisive rocks.

Nor do we drift in isolation. When, by incident or accident, some person of note swims near, we are like to reach out, to grab hold, tie them to our self as a lifeline. So it is that we, yet anchored to our positions, find ourselves bound up by others: hopeful companions and desperate rivals alike. Should we attempt to swim in another direction we may find the rope taut as they struggle against the pull. Should the waves drag them underwater, this linkage will take us with them.

So it goes until the day you find all your limbs shackled, pulled to different directions, joints wracked and puzzled. What option will you have but to take a knife and cut something loose? 

## Anchors

Every anchor describes a behavior. In a collection, they summarize much about the personality you can expect from an individual. They're the things that character is want to do: their instincts, proclivities, and compulsions.

When someone fulfills an anchor, they've acted in accord with their true nature. It will feel right: free of any internal strife. Not to say other behaviors, any which don't match to an anchors, beget dissonance or frustration. Rather, it's quite the opposite. In a pocket, surrounded by psychic noise, being yourself can prove to be a very grounding experience.

Groundedness is tracked very literally. In every Ego playbook, you'll see a meter like this:

### Fulfilling your anchors

Every PC will begin with ten total anchors: five from their Self, and five from their Ego. Don't fret if that sounds like a lot! If this is your first character, you'll only consider one at a time.

Characters don't juggle all their anchors at once. Just like a real person, there's only a select few sitting atop your mind (whether or not you realize it): the behaviors you really need to embrace once again. These anchors are _active_. Your active anchor is always marked, and only your active anchors can be fulfilled.

<ul class="anchor-list">
  <li class="anchor-active">Give someone a compliment. (active)</li>
  <li class="anchor-li">Visit justice upon the cruel. (inactive)</li>
</ul>

What then?

> ####  Fulfill an anchor
>
> When you **clearly and consciously act in line with one of your active anchors,** you've fulfilled that want. First, take one step towards `Grounded`. Second, deactivate this anchor and activate a different one. Finally, move your `Confidence` and `Hesitance` (if unlocked) each to a different anchor.

Fulfilling an anchor is a lot like it sounds: you took action in line with your wants. Two things happen: first, you're feeling more like your own self again. Go back to your grounded tracker. You'll either move from `Adrift` to the center, or from the center to `Grounded`.  If you're already grounded, you'll remain as you are. That's fine. You're exactly where you need to be. Second, you'll have to shift around your anchors a bit. Now that you've appeased this piece of your mind, another want is likely to bubble up and take its place.

### Grounded and Adrift

These two states are one of many ways we get to peek at your character's mental and emotional status. When you're grounded, you feel like yourself. When you're adrift, your feel addled and unlike yourself.

Side note: you aren't expected to play out these states at any time in the conversation. Groundedness is an ever-shifting humor. The exploration is all in how you take yourself through those transitions, not in how you project a current mood. Let the mechanics lead you through.

When adrift, you'll want to lean into your anchors and settle your feet back on the firm earth again. But what about when you're already grounded? In that case, you have some freedom to work against yourself without risking self harm.

> ####  Disharmonize
>
> When you **commit to a personal schism** and act against your nature or bindings, if you are `Grounded` you can step to `Adrift` to either reroll all the dice in a play, or ignore the constraints of a locked binding. Otherwise, you can take one step towards `Adrift` to ignore an unlocked binding, disregard your `Hesitance`, or use a burdened play without invoking the burden.

### Confidence and Hesitance

Just as your anchors themselves inevitably shift and change, so to will your sense of assurance in your own predispositions. The compulsion to act a certain way is divergent from any wind of courage or pang of worry when it comes time to do the act. We represent these feelings with confidence and hesitance.

As your character progresses you'll eventually mark one of your active anchors as `Confident` and another as `Hesitant`. How exactly you want to add this mark is up to you. Mostly because I haven't figured out a satisfactory ux for it. Something as simple as a plus and minus can be sufficient.

<ul class="anchor-list">
  <li class="anchor-active"> Give someone a compliment. (confident)</li>
  <li class="anchor-active"> Visit justice upon the cruel. (hesitant)</li>
</ul>

While marked one way or the other, if you use a play to fulfill an anchor (and roll the dice in the process), you'll take on the associated effect for that play. Keep in mind that it's the trigger for the play which counts toward fulfilling the anchor, not the results of the play; the position always applies.

## Bindings

> only draft notes for now

A binding states how you're tied to someone else. You can phrase it as simply as two words: their name, and your feelings. For example, `Hate, Jon` is a binding stating that you hate Jon. I doesn't have to be two words. But it doesn't have to be prose, either.

Bindings can express positive or negative associations. `Love, Erica` is as valid as `Fear the worm at the center`.

### Binding and Anchors

Every binding gets set on an Anchor. There's not a lot of space on the character sheet to do this. It's easier with a notepad or sticky notes. It doesn't matter whether the anchor is active or not, the binding always applies.

What does this do? I haven't fully figured it out yet. What I want to do is channel the sensibility of the effects of UHH's playbook questions (ex: "I can never put them off or waylay them.") It's a beautiful little contract that, well, isn't very easy to remember, much less change (outside of just writing in the change; y'know, fair game).

First try is bindings as emotive bonds, attached to anchors.  If that doesn't work, I might try attaching them to moves (more directly like UHH) instead.

### Locking and breaking

Every binding has two stages: unlocked, and locked.  There's no ux for locking one. I guess just circle it?

Some moves will lock an unlocked binding, others will break them. In general, if a move asks you to add a binding with a character when you already have one (or more) with them, decide first if the new binding would overlap with an existing one. If it does, and the binding is unlocked, you should lock that one first.

Breaking works in reverse. A locked binding has its lock broken. An unlocked binding that gets broken has to be removed.

This mechanic kinda serves a useful purpose. It's also clunky. Might go into the incinerator in the future.
