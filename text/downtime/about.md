# Time outside the pocket

In between their brief seances within pocket reality, the players' characters try to spend their time as any other person would: simply living. They have duties to attend, chores to manage, mouths to feed, and troubles to skirt around. Then again, that simplicity itself is a fancy. Can they possibly separate themselves from the work? Each visit to that psychic space leaves them with some new ache. If not a pained memory or a hollow space once fleetingly filled with joy, then the stains of iniquity- that very accumulation for which their clients hire them, as transitive repositories, as human cups into which the well to do can drain out the evils that plague the people they actually care for.

Enter these characters back into their home each day, burdened with a new weariness plainly visible from the distance in their clouded gazes, afflicted by another terror made audible by their barking howls throughout the night. The dream of a calm and happy life might well be a place inside their own pocket reality. That, they know, makes it all the more dangerous. Such places as that lie vulnerable. They've seen it so many times themselves. If they want to hold onto those ideals, they must take means to protect them.

## The downtime phase

Downtime is an opportunity for players to reveal something personal or new about their character; the off-stage parts of their lives, as real people. We get to delve deeper into their relationships, watch them assimilate into- or transgress against- society, and take a peek into the real person, not the Visitor they project to the pocket.

The Conductor (a temporary title given to a player while they run their scene) always takes the part of their own character. The rest of the players (including the MC) pick up the supporting parts, as many as are needed. No one takes the position of MC here; the world is momentarily under the Conductors direction.

Every downtime phase follows these same steps:
1. Each of the players picks the kind of scene they want to run on their turn.
2. One at a time (in no particular order) the players take the role of a **Conductor** and runs the table through their scene.
3. This phase concludes and play cycles back to another Seven Course Meal.

A final rule: no one may use their PC's Plays during these scenes. Those are relegated to the Pocket, and the Ego they present therein. You may use the Basic Plays as oracle or arbitration, if you must. Always roll a D6 instability, if you do. Or you can simply use a coin flip to decide, or any other method. The only rule is that you see the scene through to its conclusion.

## Conducting downtime scenes

The Conductor should always begin with talking about the idea for their scene. Not an end state- the table still plays to find out what happens- but necessarily a start: what do they want to show or explore? Must it maintain a certain tone or objective?

Everyone at the table should freely ask questions and pitch ideas before moving on to play. If the table doesn't know what's important in the scene, and what might be at stake, keep asking questions. Better to go in with more of an idea than less.

Each player (including the MC) picks an character to play through the scene. These may be their own characters, but are more likely to be an NPC: some are only bit parts, others are people of genuine importance, and lingering presence, in the Conductor's character's life.

When opening the scene, take time to describe the situation, location, characters on camera, tone, goals, and any other details that might be important to the Conductor. Try to set the scene to motion in the right direction, but don't indulge this part too much. The show must go on!
