# About Plays

## Powered By The Apocalypse

This game is a [PBtA](http://apocalypse-world.com/pbta/games/find) gig, and attributes much of its legacy to [Under Hollow Hills](https://lumpley.games/under-hollow-hills-about-the-game/). Most details from that framework hold true even here: the conversation, the plays (or moves, if you're coming from AW), and all the intertwining of the two.

## Making plays

Plays are the means by which players push and pull at the fiction, giving characters capacity to learn, communicate, relate, and struggle. Each one is a self-contained little rule, specific to its own purpose, with a guide on how to enact it and what happens as a result.

To use a play the character has to do what it says. You can't solely speak its name then skip to the consequences. Moreover, if a character does what the play says then they're enacting the play and cannot avoid it.

Players are encouraged to call out their plays by name and intent. There's no necessary order between fiction and call; you can describe what you're doing and then call out its play, or the reverse. You can even say only one of the two, and then ask for help to figure out the other. Other players may call out your play for you, if they think you missed it. Most often the MC will do so to help you out.

## Handling gaps

Every play serves to elicit a particular mood or motive. The list is finite, and it isn't difficult for a character to take an action where no play fits. These gaps are intentional. If you land in one, first see if any other play fits your intent (eg: you're not _throwing a punch_- that's not a play here- but maybe you're _Barging In_, or maybe you're getting into a _Fracas_).

When all else fails the table can fall back to the conversation. Talk about what's a stake in this moment: what you want, and where the other players are invested. No rule here states that conversation isn't enough. Plays just help guide the direction.
